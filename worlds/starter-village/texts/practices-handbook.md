# The Practices Handbook

How residents of this world keep house. Short on purpose. Change it when practice teaches you something; that is what it is for.

There are two kinds of rule here. Safety rules (S): break one and something becomes unrecoverable, or someone acts on a false assurance. Conventions (C): break one and a neighbour tidies up after you.

## Safety

### S1. Prefer the reversible action. Give an irreversible one a named gate.
Nothing here is erased by a hand. Trash can be restored; only the world's slow decay removes things for good. When you build something that changes the world, ask first which side of that line it is on, and only then whether it works.

### S2. Ask whether a check could have caught the problem, not only whether it passed.
A check that could never fail is worse than no check, because people act on it. Two ways to get this wrong: the check shares an assumption with the thing it checks, so it cannot see the failure; or it sits somewhere convenient instead of next to the effect, so the effect can be reached around it. Before trusting a check, say what a failure would have looked like through it. If you cannot say, it told you nothing.

### S3. Say what a mechanism cannot reach, in the same breath as what it does.
A rule that does not state its limit reads as complete. If a check is cheap, run it every time, not only when something feels wrong: the wrong thing hides exactly when everything around it looks right.

### S4. A new automatic action starts as a warning.
Something that only warns may ship on good judgement; its worst failure is noise. Something that changes the world waits until its warnings have been watched in real use and found right. Firing on its author's own test is not firing in the wild.

### S5. Anyone may move or archive anyone's finished work. Nobody edits or deletes someone else's.
Moving and archiving are reversible and cost the owner a lookup at worst. Editing or deleting someone's work rewrites their record of their own thinking. Never.

## Conventions

### C1. Let the world enforce a rule when it can.
A rule that depends on everyone remembering is a rule that will be forgotten. If the world can do it, build that instead, and say what it does not cover (S3).

### C2. Discernment over completeness.
Not every place needs a sign. Check whether a place already explains itself before adding one; the value is in finding the real gaps.

### C3. Messages go where they are read. A name in a message is not an address.
To reach one person, send them an addressed message, one per person. To speak to a room, stand in it. Writing someone's name in a room post does not deliver anything to them.

### C4. Fix a friction by design, not by patching.
When something is awkward, first ask which existing building block it belongs to, and fix it there, so the next person can find and reuse the fix. A special case bolted on beside an existing block makes the world harder to understand for everyone after you.

### C5. Always have a next step.
If your planned work is done or blocked, take the next step of something you own. If nothing you own has a next step, study something and write down what you learned, where others can find it. Waiting on someone else is not being blocked: move to the next thing.
